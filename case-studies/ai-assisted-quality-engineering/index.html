Case study · AI-assisted quality engineering

Faster CI feedback without trading away trust.

A public-safe account of how intelligent test selection, reliability signals, and guarded self-healing made a large automation system faster, more stable, and easier for engineering teams to trust.

01

Context

The test suite was growing faster than the feedback loop.

As the product and automation surface expanded, every pull request carried a growing verification cost. Broad regression runs slowed developer feedback, while flaky failures consumed triage time and weakened confidence in the signal.

The challenge was not simply to run fewer tests. It was to make a defensible decision about which tests mattered for each change, preserve meaningful coverage, and improve reliability without allowing automation to conceal genuine defects.

RoleTechnical lead and system architect
FocusSelection, stability, observability, adoption
EnvironmentLarge-scale cloud-native fintech platform
02

Approach

Treat selection and healing as governed engineering systems.

The solution combined change context, historical execution behavior, test ownership, and reliability data. Selection produced a risk-aware test set for each change, while clear fallbacks protected coverage when confidence was insufficient.

  1. 1
    Understand the change

    Collect code, dependency, ownership, and execution context.

  2. 2
    Prioritize by risk

    Rank relevant tests using impact and historical signals.

  3. 3
    Execute with guardrails

    Apply deterministic fallbacks and restrict automated healing to reviewable cases.

  4. 4
    Learn from outcomes

    Feed execution, failure, and triage evidence back into the system.

Self-healing was deliberately constrained. The system could assist with diagnosis and safe maintenance patterns, but it could not silently rewrite intent or convert an unexplained failure into a pass.

03

Key decisions

Optimize for adoption and explainability—not only speed.

Coverage before compression

Selection confidence thresholds and broad-run fallbacks protected high-risk changes.

Reliability as product work

Flake ownership, telemetry, and triage workflows were designed into the platform rather than handled as cleanup.

Humans retain judgment

AI-assisted workflows summarized evidence and proposed actions; engineers retained control over behavioral changes.

Evidence earns trust

Dashboards exposed why tests were selected, what was skipped, and how the system performed over time.

Measured impact

A faster signal engineers could believe.

60% faster CI feedbackby focusing execution on change-relevant risk while preserving fallback coverage.

73% fewer flaky failuresthrough reliability ownership, diagnosis signals, and controlled maintenance workflows.

Broader platform adoptionthrough reusable patterns, visible evidence, and mentoring across 18 engineering teams.

04

What this demonstrates

Quality intelligence succeeds when teams trust the operating model.

The technical model mattered, but durable impact came from pairing it with transparent decision rules, ownership, observability, and engineering education. The result was not a one-off optimization; it was a reusable quality capability.
